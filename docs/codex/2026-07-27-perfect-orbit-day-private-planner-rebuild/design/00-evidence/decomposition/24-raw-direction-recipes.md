# Twenty-four raw experience directions

Status: generative divergence complete; no winner implied by numbering
Rule: each recipe changes mental model, topology or interaction—not merely palette

## Evaluation frame

Every recipe is judged against five frequent owner jobs:

1. know what matters now;
2. capture a thought in under five seconds;
3. complete/correct a task or repeated habit with one clear gesture;
4. plan a realistic day/week without losing unscheduled work;
5. inspect history, recovery and sync truth without entering edit accidentally.

It must also survive phone/tablet/Windows, dense data, 200% text, mixed Persian/
English, keyboard/IME, offline/error and reduced motion.

## R01 — Pulse and Stream

- Mental model: a compact vital sign for the day above one continuous operational
  stream.
- Topology: Today Pulse -> next boundary -> time zones -> flexible work -> habits ->
  completed fold.
- Signature interaction: the now marker and next actionable row gently trade visual
  emphasis as time/status changes.
- Material: warm paper canvas, planar rows, glass only header/footer/capture.
- Strength: fastest orientation and direct execution; removes Orbit duplication.
- Risk: can look like a generic list unless timeline, typography and state receipts
  are crafted exceptionally well.

## R02 — Living Ledger

- Mental model: a personal chronological journal where plans and outcomes become an
  immutable daily ledger.
- Topology: date chapter, planned entries, live log receipts, reflection margin.
- Signature interaction: every completion/correction appends a small auditable event
  that can expand into history.
- Material: editorial rules, tabular numerals, subtle paper depth.
- Strength: excellent trust, correction and history model.
- Risk: may feel administrative and too dense for playful habit motivation.

## R03 — Day Rooms

- Mental model: morning/afternoon/evening are spatial rooms the owner moves through.
- Topology: one active room full-width; adjacent rooms peek and can be swiped/clicked.
- Signature interaction: current room expands while future/past compress without
  hiding tasks.
- Material: three authored pastel environments, not cards.
- Strength: emotional time orientation and one-handed focus.
- Risk: non-time-specific and cross-room work can become awkward.

## R04 — Ribbon Planner

- Mental model: a single continuous ribbon of time and commitments.
- Topology: vertical phone ribbon; horizontal desktop ribbon with stacked conflicts.
- Signature interaction: pinch/wheel changes time density while preserving now.
- Material: textile-like strokes and layered labels.
- Strength: strong relationship between schedule, duration and free space.
- Risk: accessibility and dense overlap complexity; habits without time need a side
  lane.

## R05 — Pocket Command

- Mental model: type/speak an intention, then command the planner.
- Topology: command surface first; contextual results/actions beneath; workspaces are
  saved query views.
- Signature interaction: natural language creates, finds, plans or updates with a
  proposal preview.
- Material: minimal console with Perfect pastel syntax tokens.
- Strength: extremely fast for an expert owner and Windows keyboard use.
- Risk: weak browseability, voice/privacy friction and overdependence on parsing/AI.

## R06 — Quiet Stack

- Mental model: only the next meaningful layer is expanded; everything else is a
  concise labeled stack.
- Topology: Now, Next, Later, Habits and Done accordions with remembered state.
- Signature interaction: completing the active layer lifts the next into place.
- Material: thick tactile separators and no repeated cards.
- Strength: low cognitive load and strong one-handed reach.
- Risk: concealed density can reduce planning overview and discoverability.

## R07 — Outcome Garden

- Mental model: goals are living systems; tasks/habits nourish visible outcomes.
- Topology: goal horizon on top, today's actions as roots/branches, history as growth
  rings.
- Signature interaction: link a task/habit to an outcome by dragging or relation
  picker; progress animates subtly.
- Material: authored botanical abstractions using the brand modules.
- Strength: meaning and motivation beyond checklists.
- Risk: decorative metaphor can obscure time-critical execution and violate the
  no-generic-orbit replacement goal.

## R08 — Daily Desk

- Mental model: a Windows-first work surface with inbox, plan canvas and live detail.
- Topology: resizable list / day board / inspector; phone collapses to sequential
  routes.
- Signature interaction: keyboard command palette, drag to time, persistent
  selection and contextual inspector.
- Material: restrained Fluent-adjacent panes with Perfect identity.
- Strength: powerful desktop planning and editing.
- Risk: phone can feel like a compromised derivative unless independently composed.

## R09 — Handrail

- Mental model: one thumb follows a stable vertical rail of status controls while
  content sits inward.
- Topology: status/log rail at reachable edge, titles/metadata center, details via
  body tap.
- Signature interaction: thumb taps/holds/swipes the same stable control geometry
  for task and habit outcomes.
- Material: soft physical rail with colored state pulses.
- Strength: best one-handed logging and visual alignment.
- Risk: handedness, RTL and tablet/desktop translation require careful adaptation.

## R10 — Split Lens

- Mental model: plan and reality are two synchronized lenses.
- Topology: planned timeline and actual outcomes side by side on wide screens; phone
  toggles with a connected shared marker.
- Signature interaction: drag a carried/missed item from actual back into a future
  plan with explicit recovery meaning.
- Material: paired mint/apricot lanes and graphite truth markers.
- Strength: excellent review, estimation learning and recovery.
- Risk: too analytical for everyday quick execution.

## R11 — Ritual Chapters

- Mental model: morning plan, daytime execution and evening review are chapters.
- Topology: current chapter opens by default; ordinary work remains accessible.
- Signature interaction: optional short guided ritual creates a feasible daily
  commitment and closes with reflection.
- Material: editorial chapter plates and quiet illustrations.
- Strength: supports intentional planning and learning loops.
- Risk: ritual fatigue; must never gate Today.

## R12 — Time Blocks First

- Mental model: every meaningful commitment occupies space in the day.
- Topology: day grid is home; unscheduled tray and habits dock around it.
- Signature interaction: capture enters tray, drag/keyboard command schedules,
  conflicts negotiate inline.
- Material: crafted translucent time blocks over a stable grid.
- Strength: realistic capacity and focus.
- Risk: unscheduled/personal habits and tiny phone screens suffer.

## R13 — Intent Map

- Mental model: tasks, habits, notes and goals form a navigable relationship map.
- Topology: active goal/project at center, related actions around it; Today is a
  filtered slice.
- Signature interaction: zoom from horizon to project to task detail.
- Material: structured nodes/paths using brand geometry.
- Strength: powerful long-term sensemaking and AI context.
- Risk: graph navigation is slower than lists and can become visual noise.

## R14 — Focus Relay

- Mental model: the day is a relay of intentional focus sessions, not a pile of
  tasks.
- Topology: current focus, queued handoff, flexible backlog and recovery lane.
- Signature interaction: finishing/reflection hands the baton to the next chosen
  block.
- Material: dynamic handoff band with restrained pulse.
- Strength: excellent execution and focus linkage.
- Risk: planning and lightweight errands become secondary.

## R15 — Rhythm Bands

- Mental model: recurring behavior is a set of weekly/monthly rhythms layered with
  one-off work.
- Topology: habit bands plus task markers across a date strip; Today expands below.
- Signature interaction: rapid logging directly in the band with streak/recovery
  receipts.
- Material: crafted woven pastel bands.
- Strength: habits and recurrence become first-class.
- Risk: task-heavy days and detailed timing are underrepresented.

## R16 — Calendar Fold

- Mental model: day, week, month and horizon are folded views of one continuous plan.
- Topology: a shared date spine expands from day list to week board to month density.
- Signature interaction: selected date and item remain anchored through scale/view
  transformation.
- Material: paper-fold depth and connected transitions.
- Strength: excellent orientation across planning horizons.
- Risk: complex animation/state preservation and possible visual gimmickry.

## R17 — Progressive Canvas

- Mental model: a blank personal canvas reveals tools only in response to content.
- Topology: empty state starts with capture; modules appear as goals, habits and
  schedules become relevant.
- Signature interaction: owner pins/reorders modules within safe adaptive regions.
- Material: quiet canvas and authored modules.
- Strength: personal and low-noise for a fresh owner.
- Risk: customization burden, inconsistent layouts and difficult support/testing.

## R18 — Commitment Queue

- Mental model: a priority queue with explicit readiness and energy rather than time.
- Topology: Ready now, Waiting, Scheduled, Low energy, Done.
- Signature interaction: filters current context/energy and promotes the next best
  action.
- Material: compact tokens and strong typographic hierarchy.
- Strength: handles unscheduled work and decision fatigue.
- Risk: calendar/time blocking and habitual rhythm become secondary.

## R19 — Storyline

- Mental model: the day tells a narrative of intention, action, interruption and
  recovery.
- Topology: narrative events along a line with task/habit chapters and notes.
- Signature interaction: interruption or plan change is logged as context, allowing
  AI to explain patterns later.
- Material: editorial timeline with expressive but sparse graphics.
- Strength: rich personal memory and intelligent review.
- Risk: too much logging and privacy-sensitive narrative detail.

## R20 — Adaptive Instrument Cluster

- Mental model: a small set of purpose-built instruments, each replacing a generic
  dashboard card.
- Topology: Today Pulse, Day Stream, Capture, Focus and Review instruments; only
  context-relevant instruments appear.
- Signature interaction: each instrument expands in place into its working surface.
- Material: tactile controls, precise scales and crafted assets.
- Strength: distinctive Perfect identity and modular cross-platform translation.
- Risk: can devolve into a card dashboard unless instruments share one flow and
  avoid duplicated metrics.

## R21 — Constraint Negotiator

- Mental model: the planner continuously protects time, energy and recovery
  constraints.
- Topology: plan plus visible capacity/constraint rail and conflict queue.
- Signature interaction: AI/manual planning proposes tradeoffs before overload.
- Material: calm constraint bands, no alarmist red walls.
- Strength: realistic planning and agentic assistance.
- Risk: complex setup and false precision without enough history.

## R22 — Habit Cockpit

- Mental model: habits are controllable systems with build/maintain/quit modes.
- Topology: today's direct controls, risk/recovery, then trends and experiments.
- Signature interaction: method-specific control becomes the visual anchor of each
  habit row/detail.
- Material: compact dials/steppers with brand craft.
- Strength: exceptional habit UX and repeated-count handling.
- Risk: tasks/goals feel bolted on if this becomes the whole product shell.

## R23 — Searchable Memory

- Mental model: every plan, note, outcome and AI decision is a private searchable
  memory.
- Topology: universal search/query first, timeline of matching entities/events,
  contextual detail.
- Signature interaction: natural language filters and saved views.
- Material: high-density editorial search results.
- Strength: powerful long-term retrieval and agent context.
- Risk: weak daily orientation and onboarding.

## R24 — Daylight Stage

- Mental model: the current day is a theatrical stage; only active commitments are
  foregrounded while future work waits backstage.
- Topology: active scene, next cue, backstage tray and completed archive.
- Signature interaction: plan/capture moves an item onstage; completion makes a
  subtle scene transition.
- Material: authored light/shadow zones and pastel spot cues.
- Strength: bold, emotional and focused without an Orbit.
- Risk: metaphor may become ornamental and hide dense planning needs.

## Forced-divergence coverage

- Radical rebuild: R03, R07, R13, R16, R24.
- Operationally calm: R01, R02, R06, R18.
- Windows-first: R05, R08, R12, R23.
- Mobile one-handed: R01, R06, R09, R14.
- Habit-first: R15, R22.
- Review/goal-first: R07, R10, R11, R19, R21.

## First rejection pass

Reject as whole-product direction, but retain useful sub-patterns:

- R07 and R13: metaphor/graph cost is too high for daily execution.
- R12: time-block-first under-serves flexible work and habit logging on phone.
- R17: owner customization would create testing and consistency debt.
- R19: logging burden is too high for a frictionless private planner.
- R22: excellent habit component direction but too narrow for the complete shell.
- R24: visually bold but risks replacing Orbit with another decorative hero.

The remaining directions proceed to a scored 8-concept shortlist. Rejected recipes
may donate a bounded component behavior only when the winning system documents the
adoption explicitly.
